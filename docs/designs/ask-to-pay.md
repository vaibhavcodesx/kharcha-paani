# Design: Ask-to-pay, the payer's half of the hisaab

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-10-14
Branch: release-1.4.0
Repo: vaibhavcodesx/kharcha-paani
Status: APPROVED; Phase 1 implemented on branch feature/ask-to-pay (2026-10-14); Phase 2 deferred (see TODOS.md)
Mode: Startup

## Problem Statement
The person who usually pays first loses money. Not because the maths is hard, but because asking feels awkward and gets put off until it's too late. In the founder's words: "i didnt note the splits and mostly i paid and thus, many times i have lost money, i think that i'll tell them, but i procrastinate and then its awkward to ask, and there's no proper hisaab."

Kharcha Paani (v1.4.0) already handles the first half, recording the expense, and does it well. It does nothing for the second half, getting the money back. The only reminders in the app are the evening "log something" nudge and the v1.4 settlement-confirmation reminders, and both only reach people who have the app. There is no way to send a friend "₹640 from Saturday" with a way to pay.

## Demand Evidence
- **The founder's own losses:** repeated, unrecovered money, caused by procrastination and awkwardness rather than miscalculation.
- **Named users:** Aaru, Mukund, Mayank, plus the founder. All are college or hostel students, and none pay.
- **What the founder has seen them do:**
  - They log the moment they pay.
  - They prefer the plain-language AI entry to the form.
  - They "have become more careful coz they're able to see what they have already spent this month."
- **Missing evidence:**
  - Nobody has paid.
  - Nobody has been watched using the app cold, without the founder helping.
  - It's unknown what any of these users would miss most if the app disappeared.

## Status Quo
WhatsApp, UPI and memory. Someone pays, mentions it in the group, and others send UPI "whenever". It's free, instant and needs no install. Its cost falls entirely on the payer: forgotten amounts and asks that feel awkward. The people who owe money are happy with it, because memory forgets in their favour.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
- **User:** a college or hostel student who usually pays first and lives on a fixed monthly allowance. The first ones are Aaru, Mukund and Mayank.
- **Wedge:** works for the payer alone and needs no install on the other side:
  1. capture in seconds (AI plain language; this already exists);
  2. the app does the asking (this is new).

  The friend who owes never needs Kharcha Paani.

## Constraints
- Solo developer. Releases are signed APKs on GitHub Releases, with no Play Store listing yet.
- Any new RLS or auth policy is a hard stop for owner review, and migrations go to live only with explicit approval.
- WhatsApp does not turn `upi://` links into tappable links. A text message cannot carry a one-tap Pay button; an https page can, because a `upi://pay` link opened in Chrome on Android launches the UPI app.
- Settling must stay payer-confirmed, as in v1.4: opening a UPI app never marks anything as paid.

## Premises
1. Unlimited free entries is table stakes, not an edge. Paytm Split Bills (launched 29 Jul 2026) gives away unlimited entries, four split types, reminders and pre-filled UPI settlement. The free tier stays unlimited, but the pitch can't rest on it.
2. The wedge works for the payer alone: capture in seconds plus the app does the asking. It must work when the friend who owes never installs anything.
3. *(Revised after the cross-model challenge.)* "A money diary that also splits" is how to frame the product for students. What actually keeps people coming back, whether seeing their own monthly spend or getting money back, is **unproven**. The first build has to measure both.
4. The paid plan stays undecided. It gets decided from evidence of what students rely on, not from a feature wishlist.
5. A sideloaded APK limits reach to friends of friends. Strangers need the Play Store before growth matters.

## Cross-Model Perspective
Codex (cold read of a structured summary):
- **Steelman:** "a private money diary for the student who usually pays first … It turns that account into a polite, actionable payment request without requiring friends to install anything."
- **Key insight:** the founder's line "i procrastinate and then its awkward to ask" is the core. "Recording a debt does not recover it."
- **Challenged premise 3 (High):** seeing the monthly total may be a welcome side benefit rather than the reason people return. It would be proven right if payers keep sending requests and recovering money while people who only use the diary drift off.
- **48-hour prototype:** "Ask for this payment" → an editable, itemised, neutral message → the share sheet → a private link that needs no sign-up → the breakdown → a UPI payment link. The payer confirms receipt. Measure shares, opens, UPI taps and confirmed repayments. Skip subscriptions, new AI work, automatic WhatsApp sending, recurring reminders and the Play Store.

Outcome: the founder revised premise 3 to "the diary frame stays; what keeps people coming back is tested, not assumed".

## Approaches Considered
### Approach A: "Ask to pay" share message (minimal)
*(Summary as considered in office hours; the Recommended Approach and the Eng Review decisions below supersede its details: entry point, QR and effort.)*
- **What it is:** on a friend's balance or an expense, an "Ask for ₹X" action opens the Android share sheet. It carries a friendly, itemised message and the payer's UPI QR as an image. No server is involved.
- **Effort:** S (human: about 1 day / CC: about 30 min). Low risk.
- **Strengths:** it works offline and for local friends, and needs no new RLS.
- **Weaknesses:** there's no tap-to-pay (the friend has to save the QR and scan it from the gallery), and nothing can be measured.

### Approach B: Install-free payment request page (ideal)
*(Summary as considered in office hours; superseded by the gated Phase 2 section and deferred by eng review D1.)*
- **What it is:** "Ask for this payment" creates a request with an unguessable token. The friend opens an https link with no sign-up, sees the itemised breakdown, taps "Pay with UPI", and the payer confirms receipt in the app.
- **Effort:** M (human: about 1 week / CC: about 3–4 hours, plus the RLS review). Medium risk.
- **Strengths:** a real one-tap payment from WhatsApp, and it's measurable.
- **Weaknesses:** a new public read path, and the payer's name and UPI ID are visible to anyone holding the link.

### Approach C: Month-end "hisaab card" (rejected for now)
A monthly budget plus a shareable summary image. It was ruled out as the first build because a monthly rhythm gives too few data points before December, and asking the whole group at once risks feeling like a public callout.

## Recommended Approach
**A now, then B** (the founder's choice; the recommendation was B alone). Phase 2 is gated on evidence; see "Gate before Phase 2".

**Phase 1, patch release (Approach A):**
- **Where (D2):** an "Ask {name} to pay" button on SettleUpSheet's "{Friend} paid you" side, reached from the friend row's Settle button. It appears only on that side and only when the friend owes the payer (net balance > 0). It reuses the amount and payee the sheet already shows. The friend row only gains the status suffix " · Asked N days ago". There's no per-expense ask in Phase 1. One entry point avoids asking for more than the friend owes once offsets and partial settlements are counted.
- **Amount asked:** the pairwise net balance already shown for that friend (`LedgerRepository` person balance: what they owe me minus what I owe them, confirmed settlements only). It is never the debt-simplification suggestion. A shared group's simplifier may route payments through other people; the ask is always between the payer and this one friend.
- **Itemised lines (D7):** built from the rows `LedgerRepository.getLedgerForPerson(personId)` already produces for the friend history. Those rows are signed and dated, include guest shares ("Your guest in a group"), and count settlements only when confirmed. The app sorts them oldest first by date (an expense's transaction date, a settlement's date), breaking ties by row id, and skips rows whose `netChange` is 0 (pending or "not right" settlements). It walks them keeping a running balance and finds the last point where that balance was zero or below.
  - If the running balance at that point was below zero (the payer owed the friend, say −₹50), the list opens with "Carried over: −₹50". So the lines always start from the true balance, whether it was zero or negative.
  - It then lists every entry after that point. **Each line's sign is its effect on what the friend owes the payer:**
    - an expense where the friend owes their share: positive, "<description> (<date>): ₹<their share>";
    - an expense where the payer owes the friend: negative;
    - a confirmed settlement from the friend to the payer: negative;
    - a confirmed settlement from the payer to the friend: positive.
  - Each line uses the share amount **stored** in Room, not a recomputed division, so 100 ÷ 3 shows the stored ₹33.34 / ₹33.33 split.
  - If the walk would list more than 8 lines, the oldest collapse into "Earlier: ₹<sum>".
  - **Guarantee:** the carried-over line plus all listed lines always equal the net balance shown. `PersonLedgerTest` already asserts the history rows add up to `netBalance`; it gains guest-covered, owed-as-guest and negative-start cases. `HisaabItemiserTest` asserts that the itemised total equals `netBalance` in each of these cases: expenses only; an offsetting expense; a partial settlement from the friend; a settlement from the payer to the friend; a pending settlement (skipped); a negative starting balance (the payer owed the friend, then the friend owed more); more than 8 lines (collapsed); and a 3-way split with rounding.
- **Message (D6):** neutral, shown as a read-only preview under the QR on the sheet, and edited in the receiving app's caption or compose box. Example: "Hey Aaru, hisaab since 4 Oct: dinner ₹300, chai ₹40, minus the ₹100 you sent → ₹240. UPI: <id>. Sent from Kharcha Paani".
- **Edited amount (D4):** if the sheet's amount differs from the net balance, the QR is for the edited amount and the message adds "Asking ₹X of the ₹Y you owe" below the lines, which still add up to ₹Y.
- **QR:** made fresh for each ask and **pre-filled with the amount**, provided the Assignment's UPI test shows that at least one major UPI app completes an amount-filled QR payment to a personal UPI ID (a warning shown before completing still counts as completing). Only if no major app completes it does Phase 1 ship an **amount-less QR** (UPI ID and name only, `UpiLink.build(upiId, name)`), and the amount appears only in the message text. The choice is made at build time from the test result. The amount-filled QR uses `UpiLink.build(upiId, name, amount, note = "Kharcha Paani hisaab")`, the same `upi://pay` URI with `am`/`tn`, encoded by the existing `qrMatrix()` (`ui/components/UpiQr.kt:56`) and drawn dark on white by a new `qrBitmap()` beside it. The URI goes only into the QR image, never into the text, because WhatsApp doesn't make it tappable. The friend scans it or uses "scan from gallery".
- **Sharing the image:**
  - `qrMatrix()` is drawn to a `Bitmap` without going through Compose.
  - The PNG goes to `cacheDir/asks/ask-<personId>.png`, overwritten on each ask so the cache doesn't grow. If writing it fails, the ask is shared as text only.
  - It's shared through the FileProvider the app already declares (`${applicationId}.fileprovider`). An `asks/` cache path is added to `res/xml/file_paths.xml`, and that file's "only CSV exports are shared" comment is updated.
  - The intent is `ACTION_SEND` with type `image/png`, `EXTRA_STREAM` = the content URI, `EXTRA_TEXT` = the message (the same text as the preview), and `FLAG_GRANT_READ_URI_PERMISSION`. It opens through `Intent.createChooser(target, title, pendingIntent.intentSender)` (D5). The `PendingIntent` must be `FLAG_MUTABLE` on API 31+, or the chosen app isn't reported.
  - **To check on a device before merging:** WhatsApp, Telegram and SMS keep the text as the image caption. If the check shows WhatsApp drops it, the app always copies the message to the clipboard before opening the share sheet and shows "Message copied, paste it under the image". That's decided at build time; the app can't detect it while running.
- **Local friends:** works the same way; the ask needs only the payer's UPI ID.
- **Payer has no UPI ID:** before sending, the app shows the payer an inline prompt to add one. If they skip it, the message has no UPI line and no QR, and still sends.
- **Ask log:**
  - A new local Room table `payment_asks(id, personId, amount, sentAt)`, needing Room **v8→v9**: a Migration, the `9.json` schema and a migration test, per the project's no-destructive-migration rule. It is **not synced**.
  - **When a row is written (D5):** a non-exported `BroadcastReceiver` writes it when the user picks an app in the share sheet. Closing the sheet without picking writes nothing. `amount` is the amount actually asked (D4).
  - The friend row shows "Asked 3 days ago" for the latest ask made after the last zero point, so the label naturally disappears once the friend is settled. It's derived, so no reset logic is needed. It's computed only for friends who have an ask row, so the cost grows with asked friends, not all friends.
- **Effort, revised:** S–M (human: about 2–3 days / CC: about 2 hours). That covers the itemisation walk and its test, the bitmap and FileProvider share, the Room migration, and the device check.

**Gate before Phase 2:** build Phase 2 only if all three of these hold:
1. The UPI test in The Assignment has been run and its results recorded **separately for the link path and the QR path**.
2. At least 3 of the 4 named users report sending an ask.
3. At least half of the reported asks led to repayment within 7 days.

Test 1 decides what the page looks like:
- **The link path** (a `upi://pay` button tapped on a web page opened from WhatsApp) completes a payment in at least one major app: the page includes the one-tap Pay button.
- **Only the QR path** works: no Pay button. The page becomes "breakdown + QR + copy UPI ID".
- **Neither** works with an amount: the page shows the breakdown, an amount-less QR and "Copy UPI ID", with the amount in plain text.

**Phase 2, next minor release (Approach B), deferred (eng review D1) to its own eng review once the gate passes; see TODOS.md:**
- **Needs sign-in.** Requests live in Supabase. In local-only mode, offline, or if creating the link fails, the ask falls back to the Phase 1 text and QR, with the notice "Sent without a link".
- **Server side, all part of the owner RLS review before live (hard stop):**
  - **Table `payment_requests`:**
    - Columns: token, payer id, **payer_first_name, payer_upi_id**, friend's display first name, amount, the friend's own lines (description, date, amount), created_at, expires_at, revoked_at, first_opened_at, first_tapped_at.
    - The payer's name and UPI ID are **copied in when the request is created**. The public read never touches `profiles` or any other table. A live link keeps showing the UPI ID it was created with; if the payer changes theirs, they send a new ask, which revokes the old link.
    - **No anonymous `SELECT` on the table.** The payer can read and write only their own rows.
  - **`get_payment_request(token)`:** a `SECURITY DEFINER` function that returns one row only by exact token match, and only if it isn't revoked or expired. Expiry and revocation are checked **on the server**.
    - It returns only these columns from that one row: `payer_first_name`, `payer_upi_id`, amount, and the friend's own lines.
    - **No other participants' names or shares** (a 4-way dinner shows "Dinner: your share ₹300").
  - **`mark_payment_request(token, kind)`:** `SECURITY DEFINER`, with kind either "opened" or "tapped". It only sets `first_opened_at` or `first_tapped_at` if that value is still empty and the request is live. Each request records each event once as a timestamp, not a counter, so repeated calls can't inflate anything.
  - **Token:** 128 random bits (`gen_random_bytes(16)`, base64url, 22 characters), generated on the server.
- **The page:**
  - Minimal and mobile-first, served with `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` and `Cache-Control: no-store`.
  - Shows the breakdown, the amount and a "Pay ₹240 with UPI" `upi://pay` button.
  - **Always** shows the amount-filled QR and a "Copy UPI ID" control, for iPhones, desktops, phones without a UPI app, or a UPI app that refuses the link.
  - Shows the button only on Android user agents.
- **What counts as opened or tapped:** "opened" means the page's script ran after rendering and called `mark_payment_request(token,'opened')`. Link-preview fetchers don't run scripts, so previews aren't counted. "Tapped" is recorded when the Pay button is pressed. The payer's own check of the link may still count as opened, which is accepted at this scale. The app shows the payer an in-app preview so they have no reason to open the link.
- **The message:** Phase 1's message plus the https link. The QR image is still attached.
- **Confirming payment:** the payer confirms receipt in the app, reusing the v1.4 settlement model. Tapping Pay never settles anything.
- **One live request per friend, a snapshot that goes stale:**
  - Making a new ask revokes the friend's previous request.
  - Any change to the payer–friend balance revokes the live request. That includes a new expense, an edit or deletion of a listed expense, and a settlement of any amount, **whoever made the change**.
    - **How it's detected:** the payer's device re-checks every live request after each local write and after each sync pull, so edits by the friend or another group member count too.
    - The re-check recomputes the friend's net balance and itemised lines and compares them with the request's snapshot (amount + lines). On any difference it queues a revocation.
    - No server-side trigger is used, so the RLS review scope doesn't change.
  - A revoked or expired link (30 days by default) shows a neutral page with no amount and no button: "This request is no longer active. Ask <first name> for the latest."
  - A revocation made offline is queued through the existing sync engine, and the old link stays live until it's sent. That's acceptable because nothing settles without the payer confirming.

## Open Questions
1. Would any student pay, and for what? (₹49 was asked and not answered.) This is deferred by premise 4.
2. Which keeps people coming back: their own monthly spend view, or recovered money? The Assignment gives the first answer; Phase 1 and 2 data add to it.
3. Should the app suggest when to ask (for example, a gentle "Aaru has owed ₹340 for 5 days" prompt to the payer)? This was left out of both phases on purpose until it's known whether a prepared ask is enough.
4. **The UPI ID is always exposed** to anyone holding the Phase 1 message or the Phase 2 link, because a UPI payment needs it. What's still open is how much else to show: the payer's full name or first name only, and whether the expense descriptions in the friend's own lines are fine to show.
5. Where does the request page live: a Supabase Edge Function, or a static page that calls the two RPCs? To be decided in /plan-eng-review.
6. Play Store timing (premise 5): before or after Phase 2?

## Success Criteria
**How it's measured.** With 4 users and the ask log on each phone, measurement is a **weekly check-in by hand**: each of the four reads off their "Asked …" entries and whether the friend paid back. That includes the founder, who can also see their own phone directly. Phase 2 adds `first_opened_at` / `first_tapped_at` from the server for the founder's own requests and anyone who agrees to share theirs.
- **Phase 1:** within 2 weeks of release, at least 3 of the 4 named users report sending an ask, and at least half of the reported asks were followed by repayment within 7 days.
- **Phase 2:** at least 50% of requests reach "opened", at least 30% of opened requests reach "tapped", and receipt is confirmed within 7 days for most of those.
- **Premise 3, judged by what people say:** with n = 4 no comparison of numbers means anything. The answer comes from the Assignment's interview question, asked again after Phase 1, plus whether the asks are actually used. More evidence only comes with more users (premise 5).

### R3-1 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> The Phase 1 QR decision rule has overlapping conditions. The amount-filled QR ships if at least one major app 'completes an amount-filled QR payment ... without refusing', and the amount-less QR ships 'if every app refuses or warns'. A plausible test result, where every app shows a warning (for example a personal-account caution on a P2P payment with an amount) but at least one still completes, satisfies both conditions, so the rule picks both QRs. That is the outcome the build-time decision exists to settle, and the rule doesn't settle it.

**Remedy**

> Make the two conditions exclusive and say how a warn-but-complete result counts. For example: amount-filled if at least one major app completes the payment (with or without a warning); amount-less only if no major app completes it. Alternatively, treat any warning as a failure in both sentences.

### R3-2 — consistency (minor)

**Problem**

> The gate now says test 1 decides the page layout, including a 'Neither' variant with an amount-less QR and no Pay button. The unchanged Phase 2 page spec still says, without conditions, that the page shows a 'Pay ₹240 with UPI' button and '**Always** shows the amount-filled QR'. The gate's precedence statement makes the intent recoverable, but the page section reads as unconditional.

**Remedy**

> Describe the page spec as the link-path variant and point to the gate for the QR-only and Neither variants, or drop 'Always' and 'amount-filled' from the page bullets.

### R3-3 — clarity (minor)

**Problem**

> In the QR bullet, 'It uses UpiLink.build(upiId, name, amount, note = ...)' comes straight after the amount-less fallback sentence. 'It' reads as the fallback, even though the fallback uses UpiLink.build(upiId, name).

**Remedy**

> Put the amount-filled construction detail before the fallback sentence, or start the sentence with 'The amount-filled QR uses ...'.

### R3-4 — completeness (minor)

**Problem**

> Two of the three Phase 2 page variants have no Pay button, so 'tapped' is never recorded for them. The Phase 2 success criterion 'at least 30% of opened requests reach tapped' then can't be met, and nothing says how those variants are judged.

**Remedy**

> Give a success criterion for the no-button variants (for example, opened rate plus receipt confirmed within 7 days, or a 'copied UPI ID' event), or state that the tap criterion applies only to the link-path variant.

### R3-5 — completeness (minor)

**Problem**

> Gate 1 now requires the link-path and QR-path results to be recorded separately, but the Assignment only says to 'note which apps complete ... which warn, and which refuse', without asking for a result per path. The gate's link-path rule ('completes a payment in at least one major app') also doesn't say how a warning counts.

**Remedy**

> Have the Assignment record complete/warn/refuse per app for each path (link, QR), and say how a warning counts in the gate's link-path rule, matching the fix chosen for R3-1.

### R3-6 — feasibility (minor)

**Problem**

> The device-side re-check needs a local list of live requests and their snapshots (amount and lines) to compare against. The only local ask table, payment_asks(id, personId, amount, sentAt), holds neither the token nor the lines. The design doesn't say whether live requests are kept in a local table or fetched from payment_requests before each check, or how this works after a reinstall or on a second device.

**Remedy**

> Say where the re-check reads live requests and snapshots from: a local cache, filled on create and refreshed on pull (a Room schema change with its own migration), or a server read of the payer's own rows. Leave the details to eng review.

### R3-7 — completeness (minor)

**Problem**

> Edits made by the friend or another group member are detected only when the payer's app next does a sync pull. Until then the link keeps showing a stale amount. Background sync is unreliable while the app is backgrounded, so this window can be days. The design accepts the offline-revocation window explicitly but not this one.

**Remedy**

> State the remote-edit window as an accepted residual risk alongside the offline-revocation one, giving the same reason (nothing settles without the payer confirming).

### R3-8 — consistency (minor)

**Problem**

**Remedy**

> Either revoke the payer's live requests when the UPI ID changes (the device-side re-check can do this), or prompt the payer to re-send open asks.
