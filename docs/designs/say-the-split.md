# Design: Say the split

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-10-26

## Problem statement

People who use Kharcha Paani every day add expenses from the home-screen widget, by voice or by typing,
within seconds of paying. But quick add doesn't understand who an expense was shared with: the AI prompt
knows nothing about friends or groups, so "dinner 1800 with Rohan and Ankit" is saved as your own
expense. Every shared expense then has to be opened again and its split fixed by hand.

## Demand evidence

- The owner's two closest friends use the app full time. Both have the widget on their home screen and
  add through its mic or text field. They often add the owner's expenses too.
- What surprised the owner: "Whenever they do an expense, they edit it directly, or they edit the
  split directly."
- Live data (read-only aggregate, 2026-10-26): since 2026-09-14, 19 of 119 group expenses (16%) were
  edited within 30 minutes of being added, and 46 more were edited later. `transactions.updated_at`
  also moves on some sync writes, so this is an upper bound, but it's the baseline to beat.

## Status quo

Before Kharcha Paani, the owner's group used WhatsApp. Anything written there got paid; the losses
came from expenses nobody wrote down, plus maths mistakes. Kharcha Paani fixed the maths and the
messy text. What's left is capture: writing the expense down completely while it's happening.

## Premises (owner agreed, 2026-10-26)

1. Capture is the product: for people who already use it, Kharcha Paani lives in the widget, so faster
   and fuller capture comes before budgets, nudges or more Insights.
2. The money leaks at the moment of spending, so capture speed is worth more than more analysis.
3. The split belongs in the capture, not after it.
4. Design for the two friends who use it daily, not for the owner, and watch them use it.

## Approaches considered

- **A. The widget remembers the split** (chips for recent groups or people on the widget). Not
  chosen: it helps only when the split repeats, and the widget has room for two or three chips.
  Worth a small follow-up after B.
- **B. Say the split.** Chosen.
- **C. Add without opening the app** (a small window over the home screen). Not chosen: it doesn't
  fix the split, and a second entry point has to start the database and session on its own, which
  is where background work has hung before.

## The design (approach B)

### What the person does

From the widget's mic or text field, or quick add anywhere in the app:

- "dinner 1800 with Rohan and Ankit": an equal split between you, Rohan and Ankit.
- "petrol 500 Goa trip": a split in the Goa trip group, among all its members.
- "chai 40": your own expense, as today.

The quick-add sheet opens already filled in, with the split shown as chips ("You, Rohan, Ankit ·
equal", or "Goa trip · 4 people"). Tapping a chip opens the existing split editor. **Nothing is ever
saved without the sheet.** The person checks it and taps Save, as they do now.

### How it works

1. **The AI returns names, the phone finds the people.** The quick-add prompt (`domain/AiPrompts.kt`,
   called by `GeminiRepository`) gains two optional fields: `with` (names as said, e.g.
   `["Rohan", "Ankit"]`) and `group` (a group name as said). The friend list and group names are
   **not** sent to Gemini: the text already holds whatever the person said, and sending the list
   would hand Google more personal data than needed (the AI notice and privacy policy cover what the
   person sends, nothing more).
2. **Matching on the phone.** A new pure function (in `domain/`, unit-tested on its own) matches the
   returned names against the person's friends, guests and groups: case-insensitive, ignoring
   honorifics and suffixes ("bhai", "ji", "da"), first name or full name, and common
   transliteration spellings. Results are:
   - one match: that person;
   - several matches ("Rahul" when there are two): the chip asks "Which Rahul?";
   - no match: the chip offers "Add Rohan as a guest", using the existing guest flow
     (`docs/splitting-with-people-not-on-the-app.md`).
3. **Offline and over the cap.** `LocalHeuristicParser` (the on-phone guess used when the AI isn't
   available) learns the same "with X and Y" and "<group name>" patterns, so the split still fills
   in offline or once today's 50 quick adds are used.
4. **Defaults.** "With X" means you plus X, equally, with you as the payer. A group name means all its
   members, equally. A group's default split mode, if it has one, wins over equal.
5. **No new screens, no schema change.** It reuses the quick-add sheet, the split editor, the
   group/friend models and `save_group_expense`. The shares still have to add up exactly
   (`SplitRounding.kt`, enforced by the server since T2.1).

### Not in this version

- Who paid ("Rohan paid 500 for petrol"). Defaulting to "you paid" matches how people add their own
  spending. This is the first follow-up if the friends ask for it.
- Unequal splits by voice ("Rohan 300, me 200").
- Approach A's widget chips.

## Success criteria

- The baseline drops: the share of group expenses edited within 30 minutes of being added goes from
  16% to under 5%, in the four weeks after release (same query as above).
- In the owner's observation (the assignment below), a friend adds a shared expense from the widget
  and saves it without opening the split editor.
- No wrong-person saves reported. A mismatch the person catches on the sheet is fine; a silent one
  isn't.

## Tests

- The matcher: a table of inputs (exact, case, honorifics, two Rahuls, unknown name, group name,
  a name that is also a group) against a real in-memory Room database of friends and groups.
- The prompt reply: the existing fake-Gemini pattern (`GeminiAiPathsTest`) with `with` and `group`
  fields, and with a reply that has neither (today's shape must keep working).
- The on-phone parser: the same phrases offline.
- One ViewModel test from text to a filled sheet whose split adds up, building the real
  `MainViewModel` (§1 rule 7: test the gatherer, not the format).

## Distribution

The next app release (1.6 or 1.7) through the existing `release.yml`, then the owner's phone test,
then Play once the store listing is up. The widget needs no change, so placed widgets keep working.

## The assignment

Before any code: sit with one of the two friends who use it daily (whoever adds the most) the next
time they pay for something shared. Don't help. Watch them add it from the widget and write down three
things: how many taps from the home screen to Save, whether they open the split editor, and what they
say or type. If they type "with Rohan" or say a group name without being told, approach B is exactly
right. If they don't, tell me what they did instead.

## What I noticed about how you think

- You answered "what did WhatsApp cost you" with the precise failure, not a complaint: "once it was
  logged in WhatsApp, it was paid", and the loss was "we forget to add it". That separates a capture
  problem from a collection problem, and it's what made this design small.
- You called yourself "kind of a lazy person" and then noticed your friends add your expenses for you.
  The most useful thing in this session was you noticing that you aren't your own user.
- You pushed to move on once the problem was solved ("Kharcha Paani already solved that problem"),
  which kept the session on the next real problem instead of re-proving the old one.
